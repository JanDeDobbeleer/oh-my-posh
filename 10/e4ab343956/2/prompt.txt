## Summary of findings (full detail in `D:\oh-my-posh\.bubbletea-analysis.md`)

**1. The cost is real and large.** Overlay-stubbing all four charm consumers (`cli/font/tui.go`, `cli/auth/tui/*`, `cli/upgrade/tui`, `cli/progress`) and comparing against baseline:
- Binary: 24,398,336 → 22,560,256 bytes (**−1.84MB, −7.5%**); 16 modules leave the buildinfo, including go-runewidth, all 8 charmbracelet modules, muesli, atotto/clipboard.
- Init time (`GODEBUG=inittrace`, 15 runs): median **39.3ms → 6.95ms** (−83%); go-runewidth alone is 27-33ms.
- Wall clock (21 runs, medians, interleaved-validated): `--version` **57.1 → 16.7ms**; a real `print primary` render **141.8 → 97.7ms (−44ms, −31% per prompt)**.

**2. The TUIs are tiny in capability.** The entire interactive surface is one non-filtering select-list (font picker), spinners, progress bars, and quit keys. No text input, no altscreen, no mouse. Auth and upgrade UIs are display-only shells; upgrade's business logic is already callback-decoupled (`report.go`), and `font install --headless` already exists. Auth's HTTP device-code flows live inside the tea models and would need a ~300-line mechanical extraction.

**3-5. Design and alternatives.** Key insight: raw mode is only needed for arrow-key selection — spinners and progress bars are pure `\r`-repaint output that works in cooked mode. A replacement is ~250-300 lines with a numbered-list picker (zero new deps, zero terminal-state risk), or ~400-480 with an arrow-key list via `golang.org/x/term` (not currently linked; its only dep, x/sys, already is). Lighter third-party libraries all pull go-runewidth — the exact cost being removed. A second-binary split saves the same 40ms but adds packaging/self-upgrade/version-skew costs across every distribution channel; strictly dominated.

**Recommendation (ranked):** replace with a small in-repo cooked-mode-first `cli/ui` package (~1,080 lines deleted, ~300 added); optionally add an x/term arrow-key picker on top; reject third-party libs and the binary split.
